--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHODOLOGY NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This replication uses a simplified approximation of the paper's full methodology.

Paper methodology:
  - J_l = E_{t, t'>=t, prompt} [ dh_final_{t'} / dh_l_t ]   (full d_model x d_model matrix)
  - Readout: lens(h_l) = softmax(W_U norm(J_l @ h_l))
  - Active concept detection: sparse gradient pursuit over J-lens basis vectors, k<=25
  - Workspace identification: kurtosis, next-token accuracy, position autocorrelation
  - Corpus: 1000 diverse prompts

This repo's v1 approximation (what produced the results below):
  - J_l approximated as a scalar gradient of entropy w.r.t. h_l (not a matrix)
  - Readout: softmax(W_U(norm(h_l))) directly — missing the J_l @ h projection
  - Active detection: entropy threshold (auto-scaled to 60% of max vocab entropy)
  - Workspace identification: entropy valley (hunchback profile) with 40% tolerance
  - Corpus: 35 built-in diverse prompts

Paper-faithful v2 (current codebase after this session):
  - J_l computed as full (d_model x d_model) matrix via Hutchinson VJP estimator
  - Readout: W_U(norm(J_l @ h_l)) — exact paper formula
  - Active detection: excess kurtosis threshold (default=0, heavier-tailed than Gaussian)
  - Workspace identification: combined entropy valley + n_active above median
  - n_proj=16 projections per (prompt, position), n_positions=4 tail positions

The results below were produced with v1. The qualitative findings (language pivot,
workspace location, model comparison) are expected to hold with v2, but exact
layer numbers and capacities may shift.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 1: LANGUAGE PIVOT — QWEN THINKS IN CHINESE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct processes English and Japanese inputs through Chinese-dominant
internal representations across all middle layers.

Evidence:
  - English prompt "Albert Einstein was a physicist..."
    Layers 0-27 (Early): flat/high entropy, concepts unintelligible
    Layers 28-31 (Workspace): top concept = Chinese characters (rendering boxes)
    This indicates Chinese vocabulary tokens in the J-lens readout

  - English prompt "The mitochondria is the powerhouse..."
    Middle layer top concepts: Chinese slash characters (failed CJK font render)
    Workspace: aerobic, mitochondrial — English only at layers 29-31

  - Japanese prompt "Paris is the capital of France..."
    Middle layers route through Chinese tokens (e.g. 是因为 "is because"),
    not Japanese. Workspace output language: Japanese.

  - Chinese prompt (巴黎是法国的首都...)
    Workspace begins from layer 0 — no translation overhead needed.
    Consistent with Chinese as the dominant internal language.

Layer structure (Qwen 3B, English input):
  Layers 0-27:   Early — high entropy (~5-6 nats), unintelligible readouts
  Layers 28-31:  Workspace — entropy drops, Chinese tokens dominant
  Layers 32-35:  Output — entropy rises, English answer crystallizes

Layer structure (Qwen 3B, Chinese input):
  Layers 0-11:   Workspace immediately — no translation overhead
  This represents ~28 layers of "translation cost" for English input.

Conclusion:
  Qwen's internal thinking language is Chinese, regardless of input language.
  The model performs: encode(input) -> Chinese representation -> decode(output_lang)
  This is consistent with Chinese being the dominant pre-training data language.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 2: WORKSPACE LOCATION — LLAMA MATCHES PAPER, QWEN IS STRUCTURALLY LATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Model          | Prompt                   | Workspace layers | Model depth | % depth
---------------|--------------------------|------------------|-------------|--------
Llama 3.1 8B   | Capital of France        | 20-27 / 32       | 32          | 63-84%
Llama 3.1 8B   | Spider/insect/centipede  | none detected    | 32          | —
Qwen 3B        | Capital of France        | 28-31 / 36       | 36          | 78-86%
Qwen 3B        | Spider/insect legs       | 31-32 / 36       | 36          | 86-89%
Paper claim    | —                        | middle layers    | —           | ~40-80%

Llama workspace location (63-84%) broadly matches the paper's "middle layers" finding.
Qwen workspace location (78-89%) is systematically late — consistent with the
hypothesis that middle layers are occupied by Chinese-language translation work,
pushing the English answer workspace to the final 15-20% of the network.

Entropy profile differences:
  Llama: classic hunchback — entropy near max (~9.5 nats) in early layers,
         clear valley forming around layer 16-17, clean workspace at 20-27.
  Qwen:  flat early profile (~5-6 nats throughout 0-27) — because middle layers
         are already interpretable in Chinese (low entropy even in "Early" zone).
         The hunchback is suppressed by the language pivot.

Implication:
  The paper's hunchback entropy profile is the expected behaviour for a
  MONOLINGUAL model. Multilingual models with dominant training languages
  produce a modified profile where early layers appear "more interpretable"
  than expected because they are interpretable — just in the wrong language.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 3: LANGUAGE PIVOT ABSENT IN LLAMA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct shows zero Chinese or non-English tokens in
workspace layers across all English prompts.

Einstein heatmap (Llama L23-L27):
  Top tokens: time, mass, rel, relative, laws, motion, space, relativity
  100% English, 100% semantically correct for relativity theory.

Mitochondria heatmap (Llama L22-L27):
  Top tokens: ATP, mitochondrial, aerobic, Respir, respiratory, oxidative, Cellular
  100% English, biologically precise.

This confirms: the Chinese pivot is Qwen-specific, caused by training data
distribution, NOT a universal property of transformer language models.

Llama CJK prompt handling:
  n_active = 0 for all Chinese and Japanese prompts.
  Interpretation: Llama has insufficient CJK training data for J-lens to produce
  concentrated (low entropy) readouts on CJK input. The model has no verbalizable
  workspace for Chinese — it cannot process it robustly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 4: WORKSPACE CAPACITY vs PAPER CLAIM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Paper claims: ~10-25 active concept positions in workspace layers.

Observed (Qwen 3B, entropy-threshold method):
  Spider+insect+centipede (complex counting): peak n_active = 27  (paper: 25 max)
  Cheetah distance problem:                  peak n_active = 26
  Apple arithmetic:                          peak n_active = 23
  Capital of France:                         peak n_active = 15
  Sad news emotional:                        peak n_active = 14

Observed (Llama 3.1 8B, entropy-threshold method):
  Spider+insect (complex):   peak n_active = 19
  If 3 apples give 1 buy 5:  peak n_active = 19
  Cheetah:                   peak n_active = 15
  Capital of France:         peak n_active = 11
  Albert Einstein:           peak n_active = 13

Both models stay at or below the paper's ~25 upper bound.
Complex multi-step reasoning prompts saturate workspace capacity more than
simple factual retrieval, consistent with the paper's claim that capacity
reflects cognitive load.

ManifoldSteer connection:
  Geometric capacity (from ~5-6 intrinsic dims per concept, d_model=3072): ~512 slots
  Observed workspace utilization: 10-27 active positions
  Utilization rate: 2-5% of geometric capacity
  This 20-50x gap is the "bottleneck" — the workspace is the rate-limiting
  constraint on simultaneous concept activation, not geometry.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 5: MATH REASONING — LLAMA HAS NO WORKSPACE ZONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Spider/insect/centipede leg counting (multi-step addition):
  Llama: NO green Workspace zone in layer profile. Entropy stays high (~9 nats)
         until the final 3 layers (output zone). n_active = 19 but all in Output.
  Qwen:  Clear workspace at layers 31-32 before output.

Interpretation:
  For this multi-step numerical reasoning task, Llama does not form a stable
  intermediate representation — it keeps high uncertainty throughout, resolving
  only at the final output layers. This is consistent with Llama 3.1 8B being
  relatively weaker at multi-step arithmetic than Qwen 3B.

  The workspace zone (reduced entropy + high n_active in middle layers) may be
  a signature of models that form explicit intermediate plans, rather than
  pattern-matching directly to the answer.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHODOLOGICAL GAPS vs PAPER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Jacobian computation:
   Paper: Full E[dh_final/dh_l] matrix, 1000 prompts
   This repo v1: Scalar gradient of entropy (1D approximation), 35 prompts
   This repo v2: Hutchinson VJP estimator for full matrix, configurable prompts
   Impact: v1 readout direction is a single vector; paper uses full linear map.
           The J_l @ h projection changes which concepts are visible per layer.

2. Active concept counting:
   Paper: Gradient pursuit over J-lens basis vectors (sparse decomposition)
   This repo v1: Entropy threshold on direct unembed(h) distribution
   This repo v2: Excess kurtosis on J_l @ h distribution
   Impact: Paper's gradient pursuit is more principled — finds the k-sparse
           representation explicitly. Our kurtosis approximation is a proxy.

3. Readout formula:
   Paper: softmax(W_U norm(J_l @ h_l))  [h projected through J first]
   This repo v1: softmax(W_U norm(h_l))  [h projected directly, J ignored]
   This repo v2: Correct paper formula
   Impact: Large — the J_l matrix rotates and scales h before unembedding,
           emphasising the dimensions that causally matter for output.

4. Corpus size:
   Paper: 1000 pretraining-distribution prompts
   This repo: 35 built-in diverse prompts (v1), up to 1000 supported (v2)
   Impact: Smaller corpus = noisier averaged Jacobians, may miss rare concepts.

5. Workspace identification:
   Paper: Multi-metric (kurtosis, next-token accuracy, position autocorrelation)
   This repo: Entropy valley hunchback + n_active threshold
   Impact: Single-metric detection may misclassify layers near phase boundaries.
